PROJECTS:

Dan's mechanical experience ranges from product design to machine design. Some of these products were designed for external use, where they were released on a company catalog. Others were designed for internal use, where they were released on a production floor. When designing a product, Dan considers the KISS method, poke yoke, genchi genbutsu, ease of assembly, manufacturability, configurability, assuring that the product is designed to last, and that the product meets the function requirements of the customer.

MACHINE DESIGN:

Dan designed machines and fixtures including the weldments, sheet metal, fixtures, and all detail & assembly drawings required. He uses the ANSI dimensional standards, tolerancing (for stackup, clearance, interference, and transition fits), GD&T, coatings (for galling & galvanic corrosion prevention), surface symbols, and weld symbols. He also has experience with static / dynamic o-ring groove design (for sealing), kinematic linkage design, keying feature alignment, and designing flow paths for optimized laminar flow.

The hydrostatic tester shown below to the left was probably Dan's highlight project of his mechatronics career. It was a greenfield gem that came once in a lifetime. Dan had the liberty to design this high pressure testing machine from scratch. With supervision, he designed and created all drawings for weldments, sheet metals, fixtures, pneumatics, hydraulics, electricals, and PLC programs. There were many meetings discussing specifications with sales suppliers and visiting sites to oversea that the fabrication was to speck. It took about 1 year to design, fabricate, and release to production.

The electropolishing machine shown below to the right was another major project of Dan's mechatronics career. He did not design the entire machine, just the monobolock fixture inside it. This project required heavy understanding of CFD fluid distribution, custom o-ring seal design, thermodynamics, and tight GD&T tolerances.

When off-the-shelf hardware is not available, Dan designs his own cylinders, cams, linkages, or bearings. When designing bearings, Dan uses a series of calculators he developed to help automate some of the conventional design methods. His calculators use a series of OCR scanned values from design records, where linear / power / log regressions interpolate the data, sub-routines calculate the eigenvalues, and a series of nested IF-ELSE statements calculate the final design values. The report showing these bearing calculators can be found here: bearings.pdf.

PNEUMATIC DESIGN:

Dan also has experience designing pneumatic systems. He is familiar with designing and assembling solenoids, manifolds, fittings, plugs, silencers, speed controllers, or FRLs (Fluid Regulator Lubricator). He has experience with the products of SMC, Bimba, and Festo. He uses PneuDraw and AutoCAD to create pneumatic and P&ID schematics.

HYDRAULIC DESIGN:

Dan had a brief exposure designing hydraulic systems for one of his projects. The design process was similar to pneumatics, but the fluid viscocity was much higher, and the pressure requirements were as high as 7,000 psi. This made it a challenge in finding compatible off-the-shelf hardware.
